Call for Papers

IDEA invites work on model-facing item identifiers in Web retrieval and ranking, spanning search, recommendation, and advertising. We welcome completed methods as well as concrete operational experiences, early results, and questions that benefit from community discussion.

Topics of Interest

IDEA focuses on three areas: identifier design and learning, evaluation and comparison, and deployment and adaptation. We welcome work on raw, semantic, and hybrid IDs across search, recommendation, advertising, and other Web systems. The topics below are indicative rather than exhaustive; other applications and explorations of ID-based features and representations in Web systems are also welcome.

Identifier Design and Learning

  • Raw, semantic, and hybrid identifiers for retrieval, ranking, and generative recommendation.
  • Content-based, collaborative, and multimodal identifier construction.
  • Discrete representation learning, quantization, tokenization, and codebook design.
  • Joint optimization of identifiers with retrieval and ranking models.
  • Item-specific memorization, cross-item generalization, and integration with other item features.
  • Shared identifier spaces and representation transfer across tasks and domains.

Evaluation and Comparison

  • Controlled comparisons, ablation studies, reproducibility, and negative results.
  • Beyond-accuracy evaluation, including diversity, novelty, coverage, fairness, and user utility.
  • Cold-start and long-tail performance, robustness, and generalization across tasks and domains.
  • Identifier quality, code utilization, collisions, and representation stability.
  • Offline evaluation, online experimentation, and longitudinal assessment.
  • Computational and operational efficiency, including memory, latency, and update costs.

Deployment and Adaptation

  • Identifier and embedding staleness under catalog evolution and distribution shift.
  • Incremental ID assignment, continual learning, and refreshing outdated representations.
  • Codebook and encoder updates, re-indexing, and coordination with model retraining.
  • Backward compatibility, versioning, ID migration, and historical interaction consistency.
  • Drift monitoring, update strategies, and trade-offs between freshness and stability.
  • Industrial deployments, scalability, serving constraints, and operational lessons.

Submission Guidelines

We welcome technical studies, controlled comparisons, reproducibility work, negative results, deployment experiences, and position statements. Contributions may address any aspect of ID-based features and representations within the workshop’s scope.

  • Review: the working plan is at least three non-conflicted Program Committee reviews per submission, with an unconflicted review lead handling disagreements and organizer submissions.
  • Proceedings: accepted papers can be published in the ACM Web Conference 2027 Companion Proceedings. Authors may opt out of proceedings publication.
Submission details to be announced

Paper lengths, formatting instructions, anonymity policy, submission platform, and final publication policies will be published when confirmed. Submissions are not open yet.
