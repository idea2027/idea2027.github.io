Call for Papers

IDEA invites research on raw (atomic), semantic, and hybrid item identifiers as learned features or token sequences in Web systems. We welcome work on raw-ID embeddings, semantic codes, and hybrid representations for search, recommendation, computational advertising, and related applications.

Our focus spans the lifecycle of these representations: their construction and learning, the evidence supporting their use, and their adaptation as catalogs, interaction signals, and models change. Contributions may study any one ID family or compare multiple designs; a comparison with semantic IDs is not required. We welcome completed methods, empirical findings, deployment experiences, and early work that can benefit from community discussion.

Topics of Interest

We focus on three areas: identifier design and learning, evaluation and comparison of ID representations, and deployment and adaptation. Topics include, but are not limited to, the following. Other applications and explorations of ID-based features and token representations in Web systems are also welcome.

Identifier Design and Learning

  • Raw-ID embeddings, item-specific memorization, and integration with content and behavioral features.
  • Semantic ID construction from content, collaborative signals, and multimodal representations.
  • Discrete representation learning, quantization, tokenization, and codebook design.
  • Hybrid identifiers combining atomic and structured components.
  • Joint learning of ID representations with retrieval, ranking, or generative models.
  • Shared identifier spaces and representation transfer across tasks and domains.

Evaluation and Comparison

  • Evaluation protocols and benchmarks for ID representations, with controlled comparisons that isolate identifier choices from changes in features, models, and training.
  • Memorization and generalization properties of raw-ID embeddings, semantic codes, and hybrid representations, including cold-start and long-tail behavior.
  • Representation quality and downstream utility, including code utilization, collisions, semantic–behavioral alignment, and their relationship to retrieval and ranking effectiveness.
  • Beyond-accuracy effects of identifier design, including diversity, novelty, coverage, fairness, and user utility.
  • Robustness and stability of ID representations under catalog changes, sparse feedback, and distribution shift.
  • Efficiency trade-offs of ID representations, including embedding-table and codebook size, token-sequence length, training cost, and inference latency.

Deployment and Adaptation

  • Raw-ID embedding and semantic-code staleness as item content, interaction patterns, and catalogs evolve.
  • Incremental ID assignment and representation learning for new and changing items, including cold-start initialization.
  • Embedding refresh, semantic-code reassignment, and codebook or encoder updates, with trade-offs between freshness and representation stability.
  • Versioning, backward compatibility, and migration of ID features or tokens, preserving item resolution and historical interaction consistency.
  • Coordination of ID representation updates with model retraining, index maintenance, and serving infrastructure.
  • Continual and cross-domain adaptation of ID representations, drift monitoring, and experience from industrial deployments.

Submission Guidelines

We welcome technical studies, controlled comparisons, reproducibility work, negative results, deployment experiences, and position statements on raw, semantic, and hybrid ID representations.

Submissions are open. Submit a single PDF in English through IDEA 2027 on OpenReview. All contribution types use the same submission venue. The descriptions below explain format and publication options.

Full Papers, Short Papers, and Position Papers

For Full and Short Papers, our formatting and page limits are the same as those of the WWW 2027 main-conference Research Track. Position Papers follow the Short Paper requirements. Use the double-column ACM format described in the official formatting requirements:

  • Full Papers: up to 8 pages of main content, with references and an optional appendix bringing the total to at most 12 pages. The first 8 pages should be self-contained.
  • Short Papers: up to 4 pages in total, including references and any appendix.
  • Position Papers: follow the Short Paper format and page limit.

Controlled comparisons, reproducibility studies, and negative results are welcome as Full or Short Papers, with the same publication options as other original contributions.

Demos, Late-Breaking Results, and Negative Results for Discussion

These contributions may be submitted as 2–8-page papers, excluding references, using the ACM double-column template. Accepted contributions are intended for presentation and discussion at the workshop and will not be included in the WWW 2027 Companion Proceedings or the ACM Digital Library.

Related work that has already been published or is under review elsewhere is welcome for presentation and discussion only. Short summaries of previously published papers are also welcome within this 2–8-page format. Please disclose the work’s publication or concurrent-submission status when submitting, without identifying the authors in the review PDF.

Formatting and Double-Blind Review

Use the ACM proceedings template. For LaTeX submissions, use \documentclass[sigconf,anonymous,review]{acmart}.

All submissions undergo double-blind review. Omit author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and identifying links from the review PDF and supplementary materials. Cite your own prior work in the third person. Summaries of published work must cite the original paper without identifying the submitting authors.

The working plan is at least three non-conflicted Program Committee reviews per submission. An unconflicted review lead will handle disagreements and submissions involving organizers.

Proceedings and Publication Options

Eligible original Full Papers, Short Papers, and Position Papers will be included by default in the ACM Web Conference 2027 Companion Proceedings, subject to the conference’s publication requirements. Authors may opt out by contacting the organizers no later than February 16, 2027, 23:59 (UTC−12).

Work already published or under review at another journal, conference, or workshop cannot be included in the Companion Proceedings and must use the presentation-only option. Preprints on arXiv and similar repositories are not considered prior publication. Presentation-only contributions do not constitute a proceedings publication and may subsequently be submitted elsewhere, subject to the destination venue’s policies.

Author registration and presentation requirements will be announced when confirmed.
