Overview

Web search, recommendation, and advertising connect users to changing collections of documents, products, and media. Item identifiers shape how models share information across items, preserve item-specific distinctions, and adapt as catalogs evolve.

The First International Workshop on Item IDentifiers Design, Evaluation, and Adaptation (IDEA) brings together researchers and practitioners working on raw, semantic, and hybrid IDs. We examine three connected questions: what should IDs encode, what evidence justifies an ID choice, and what must survive when representations are updated?

Through keynote talks, contributed work, and open discussion, IDEA will bring controlled comparisons, negative results, and deployment experience into a shared conversation across search, recommendation, and advertising.

Workshop Objectives

Bring competing perspectives on item identifiers into conversation, challenge assumptions about what IDs should do, and establish the evidence needed to compare their benefits. IDEA gives equal consideration to raw, semantic, and hybrid IDs, including evidence in favor of retaining an existing design.

Call for Papers

Designing and learning

How should item IDs be constructed and used?

  • Raw, semantic, and hybrid identifier designs.
  • Content, collaborative signals, and multimodal ID construction.
  • Joint learning of identifiers and retrieval or ranking models.
  • Information sharing, item-specific distinctions, and feature-based alternatives.

Evaluating and comparing

What evidence supports an identifier choice?

  • Controlled comparisons and attribution of gains to ID design.
  • Reproducibility studies and negative results.
  • New-item and long-tail performance across search and recommendation.
  • Memory, latency, and update costs.

Deploying and evolving

How should IDs be maintained as items and models change?

  • Catalog updates, new items, and representation stability.
  • Encoder changes, code refreshes, and index maintenance.
  • Historical interactions and correct item resolution.
  • Coordinating representation updates with model retraining.

Submission Guidelines

We welcome technical studies, controlled comparisons, reproducibility work, negative results, deployment experiences, and position statements. Contributions should make a concrete identifier-design decision, comparison, or failure case clear.

  • Review: the working plan is at least three non-conflicted Program Committee reviews per submission, with an unconflicted review lead handling disagreements and organizer submissions.
  • Proceedings: eligible accepted papers may enter the Companion Proceedings, subject to the conference’s publication conditions.
  • Non-archival contributions: a separate route for position statements and discussion contributions is planned.
Submission details to be announced

Paper lengths, formatting instructions, anonymity policy, deadlines, submission platform, and final publication policies will be published when confirmed. Submissions are not open yet.

Program Highlights

A half-day, in-person workshop is planned, with substantial time for audience participation.

  • Keynote talks connecting research findings with deployment perspectives.
  • Contributed presentations including technical talks, lightning talks, and demonstrations.
  • Poster exchanges supporting informal conversations during the coffee break.
  • Brainstorming and open discussion comparing evidence and identifying useful next experiments.

Community Roadmap

IDEA aims to produce a public Community Roadmap: a short workshop report bringing together findings and deployment lessons, unresolved disagreements, priorities for follow-up studies, and shared collaboration resources.
